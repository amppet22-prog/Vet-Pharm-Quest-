## Ten name gaps resolved (Master Table pp. 2–9)
- **Epinephrine** — mixed alpha/beta adrenergic agonist; emergency use.
- **Ephedrine** — historic mixed sympathomimetic; distinguish from preferred targeted bronchodilators.
- **Codeine** — central opioid antitussive; productive-cough caution.
- **Hydromorphone** — full mu opioid; analgesia and possible feline emesis.
- **Ractopamine** — production-animal beta-adrenergic repartitioning agent; legal/market-specific.
- **Zilpaterol** — cattle-feed beta-agonist; product voluntarily withdrawn from market; serious equine hazard.
- **Tolazoline** — alpha antagonist used in certain historic xylazine reversal contexts.
- **Vatinoxan** — peripherally selective alpha-2 antagonist in FDA-approved medetomidine combination for canine sedation/analgesia.
- **Carbamazepine** — sodium-channel anticonvulsant in source; limited/nonroutine veterinary use.
- **Ketamine** — dissociative NMDA antagonist; anesthesia and selected analgesia.
- **Bismuth salicylate / subsalicylate** was a naming match rather than a missing drug. Existing shelf ID `bismuth-subsalicylate` preserved.

## Teaching and UI requirements
1. Prefer **patient data → decision → mechanism → WHY**, not one recognizable answer among unrelated distractors.
2. A good distractor should be a plausible **same-class alternative, adjacent receptor, different diagnosis or management step**, with a specific reason it is wrong.
3. Laboratory values must show **units, relevant lab reference intervals or a clearly labeled clinical threshold**, and trend when available.
4. Hide rationales until answering; label any uncertain lecture claims visibly rather than silently treating them as verified.
5. For Pharmacy shelves: drug-specific indications and pathogens where supported; do not substitute generic “consult label” for the actual indication. Continue retaining legally necessary species/withdrawal cautions.
6. Avoid resetting save keys or study mastery IDs; new drugs use additive IDs.
